## 1. Why Statistically Test Randomness? (為何需要統計隨機性檢測？)

A TRNG or DRBG can look random and still be catastrophically weak — a subtle bias, a repeating structure, or a correlation between bits is often invisible by eye but fatal to cryptographic security. **NIST SP 800-22**, *A Statistical Test Suite for Random and Pseudorandom Number Generators for Cryptographic Applications*, defines **15 statistical tests** that a candidate bit sequence must pass before it is considered suitable for cryptographic use. Unlike NIST SP 800-90B (Module 37), which estimates a conservative **min-entropy**, SP 800-22 is a **hypothesis-testing** framework: for each test, the null hypothesis $H_0$ is "the sequence is random," and a **P-value** is computed. If

$$
P\text{-value} \ge \alpha \quad (\text{conventionally } \alpha = 0.01)
$$

the sequence is deemed to have *passed* that test at the 1% significance level; a P-value below $\alpha$ rejects randomness for that specific statistical property.

## 2. The 15 Tests of the SP 800-22 Suite (SP 800-22 檢測套件的 15 項測試)

| # | Test | What It Detects |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Frequency (Monobit) Test** | Overall proportion of 0s vs. 1s deviates from 1/2 |
| 2 | **Frequency Test within a Block** | Local bias inside fixed-size blocks (global balance can hide local bias) |
| 3 | **Runs Test** | Too few/many "runs" (consecutive identical bits) — oscillation rate abnormality |
| 4 | **Longest Run of Ones in a Block** | Longest all-1 run length deviates from the expected distribution |
| 5 | **Binary Matrix Rank Test** | Linear dependence among fixed-length substrings (rank of disjoint sub-matrices) |
| 6 | **Discrete Fourier Transform (Spectral) Test** | Periodic (non-random) components via peak heights in the DFT spectrum |
| 7 | **Non-overlapping Template Matching Test** | Excessive occurrences of a specific non-periodic target pattern |
| 8 | **Overlapping Template Matching Test** | Same as above but allows overlapping matches — sensitive to different structure |
| 9 | **Maurer's Universal Statistical Test** | Whether the sequence can be significantly compressed (compressibility ⇒ non-random) |
| 10 | **Linear Complexity Test** | Whether the sequence is generated by a short Linear Feedback Shift Register (LFSR) |
| 11 | **Serial Test** | Uniformity of all possible overlapping $m$-bit patterns |
| 12 | **Approximate Entropy Test** | Compares frequency of overlapping blocks of length $m$ vs. $m+1$ |
| 13 | **Cumulative Sums (Cusum) Test** | Maximal excursion of the running sum (random walk) from zero |
| 14 | **Random Excursions Test** | Distribution of visit counts to particular states in a derived random walk (cycles) |
| 15 | **Random Excursions Variant Test** | Total visit counts to 18 states across the entire random-walk sequence |

## 3. Worked Example: The Frequency (Monobit) Test

Convert bits $\{0,1\}$ to $\{-1,+1\}$ and sum them:

$$
S_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i, \qquad x_i \in \{-1, +1\}
$$

$$
s_{obs} = \frac{|S_n|}{\sqrt{n}}, \qquad P\text{-value} = \operatorname{erfc}\!\left(\frac{s_{obs}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)
$$

A perfectly balanced sequence has $S_n \approx 0$, so $s_{obs} \approx 0$ and $P\text{-value} \approx 1$. A strongly biased sequence pushes $|S_n|$ large, driving the P-value toward 0 and failing the test.

## 4. Worked Example: The Runs Test

First checks the pre-test proportion $\pi = \frac{\#\text{ones}}{n}$; if $|\pi - 0.5|$ is too large, the runs test is not even meaningful (frequency test already failed) and it aborts. Otherwise it counts the total number of runs $V_n(obs)$ (a "run" is a maximal subsequence of identical bits) and computes:

$$
P\text{-value} = \operatorname{erfc}\!\left(\frac{\left|V_n(obs) - 2n\pi(1-\pi)\right|}{2\sqrt{2n}\,\pi(1-\pi)}\right)
$$

Too few runs suggests long streaks (low-frequency oscillation, possible stuck-at behavior); too many runs suggests unnaturally rapid alternation — both are non-random signatures.

## 5. Interpreting Results in Practice (實務判讀)

A single sequence typically must be split into many sub-sequences and each test applied repeatedly; the *proportion* of sub-sequences passing (P-value ≥ 0.01) is itself checked against a confidence interval, and the P-values' own distribution is checked for uniformity via a second-level chi-square test — guarding against a generator that passes marginally every time due to a subtle systematic bias.

SP 800-22 is normally applied **after** conditioning/whitening (Module 37 §3) as a final validation gate, and is complementary to — not a replacement for — the min-entropy estimation of NIST SP 800-90B, since passing all 15 statistical tests does not itself certify high min-entropy (a sequence can look statistically uniform while still being predictable to an attacker who knows the generating algorithm).
